# KV-cache vs fixed recurrent state: what exactness costs (Cloud 9)

Transformers keep every token. Each step writes its key and value vectors into a KV-cache, so memory grows with length — O(n) slots — and any past token stays exactly retrievable by attention. That is why long-context quality tracks cache size, and why the bill tracks it too: without eviction or compression, each new token attends over all old ones, so compute grows quadratically and serving 32K+ contexts turns into latency and OOMs. If you have run a long chat and felt it slow down, you felt the cache.

The pressure is cost per token at long context. Linear attention, selective state-space models, and fixed-size recurrent memories answer by collapsing the cache into a fixed-shape state updated as you read: O(1) memory in length, O(T) compute. BDH-CQ's paper states the cleanest version — state that accumulates additively per demonstration. The trade is sharp: keys that point in similar directions now share storage, so new writes smear old ones and verbatim recall falls once the stream outruns capacity. Nothing flags the loss. It just answers wrong.

Our page lets you feel that in under a minute. Two toys eat the same stream of pairs like `mango-3 → 42`. The cabinet (`Map`, one drawer per pair) files everything. The whiteboard (`slot = hash % N`, last writer wins, plus four more write rules you can flip between) keeps N slots total. At 120 pairs into 32 slots the whiteboard already has ~80 overwrites; flood 500 into 16 and recall craters while the cabinet stays 100% and its slot-line climbs against the flat O(1) line. Click a yellow cell and the table names the keys that stole it. That gap — truth next to estimate — is the whole point.

| Family | Memory | Verbatim recall | Bill | What it is good at |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transformer KV | O(n) drawers | perfect | O(T²), OOMs | gold standard when you can pay |
| Mamba (Gu & Dao, 2024) | O(1), input-gated | weak far back | cheap | throughput on long streams |
| HOLA (Cui, arXiv:2607.02303, 2026) | O(1) + 8-slot exact cache | recovered for surprises | cheap + cache | needle/passkey rescue |
| BDH / BDH-CQ (Pathway) | O(1) synaptic ρ, S_t | sags under load | lowest $/task we have seen | memory and reasoning in one fabric |

Three concrete efforts, not a list. Mamba showed gating can carry long sequences cheaply but verbatim needles still hurt relative to full attention. HOLA (340M params, 15B SlimPajama tokens) kept its delta-rule compressive state and added a bounded exact cache written by surprise — the committed residual β‖e‖ — cutting Wiki perplexity 27.32→22.92 and holding RULER needle recall out to 32K, 16× its train length. Its abstract says our claim back to us: O(1) memory at the cost of lossy exact memory. BDH is the brain-flavored member of the same family: no token drawers, just a synaptic matrix updated as it reads, ρ_{t−1}=Σ v*xᵀU^{t−τ} (Kosowski et al., arXiv:2509.26507, Eqs.4–5; Hebbian view 6–8; toy code App.E). BDH-GPU restates that with ReLU low-rank maps plus linear attention — not fixed-φ Performer/Katharopoulos which drifts, but sparse (~5%) non-negative activations + Hebbian plasticity + decay U for stability. It is not a Mamba-style SSM, and we do not call it one.

Evidence, with the labels judges asked for. Pathway reports a 134M BDH at 95% on BABILong QA1–QA5 at 32K, 82% at 128K (pathway.com homepage). That is a vendor benchmark, pending contamination checks, independent validation, and leaderboard review — a benchmark, not a deployment, and we present it as such. Same shape as the toy: holds long, sags loaded. Pathway also reports BDH-CQ, 150M params, at 29.5% pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 for a computed ~$0.0007/task (arXiv:2608.09888), roughly 11× cheaper per task than GPT-5.6 Luna Low at 34.2% in their table. That is a cost-efficiency (Pareto) claim on one self-reported benchmark, checkpoint unreleased — not raw SOTA, not independent. Our live curves reproduce the shape on junk words; the vendor numbers sit beside them as clearly marked precomputed cards.

BDH's role here is direct: it is the O(1)-by-design case study, synaptic state instead of slots. BDH-CQ is the sibling datapoint that the same family can trade raw accuracy for intelligence-per-dollar by reasoning in latent state without emitting chain-of-thought tokens (S_t=U(S_{t−1},D_t), no eval demos in training, no weight updates at inference). Where BDH has no role we would say so; here it is the point.

The limit we would defend first: forgetting is silent — no flag says "I overwrote that" — and remembering inside one session is not learning across sessions. Moving useful fast state into slow weights (fast-to-slow consolidation) stays open, and our toy's hash collisions are a cartoon of the real failure, which is superposition along shared key directions.
