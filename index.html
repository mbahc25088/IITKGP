BDH module · the part we actually sweated over

Okay, so the real machine did the whiteboard thing too.

Who: Dragon Hatchling (BDH), Pathway's weird-and-cool Post-Transformer. Quick note so nobody gets yelled at in Q&A: not a Mamba-style SSM — BDH-GPU is its own GPU-friendly rewrite from ReLU low-rank maps + linear attention. BDH doesn't keep drawers; it scribbles into synapses while reading. The one equation we're confident enough to frame (Eq.5):

ρt−1,l = Στ<t v*τ,l−1 xτ,lT Ut−τ — BDH-GPU attention state, Eq.5: fixed-shape matrix, additive updates, O(1) in length

Same deal as your whiteboard upstairs, just with learned keys, a decay U, and ~5% of neurons awake at a time. If you want the full math: Kosowski et al. §3.2 Eqs.4–5, Hebbian lens 6–8, toy code App.E. We read it so you can skim it.

Receipt #1 · vendor told us, we didn't verify: 134M BDH gets 95% at 32K → 82% at 128K on BABILong QA1–QA5 (pathway.com). Holds long, sags under load — exactly your toy's curve, at real scale. Pending contamination / independent / leaderboard review. A benchmark is not a deployment, we'll say it in the viva too.
Receipt #2 · the cousin BDH-CQ: 150M, 29.5% pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 @ ~$0.0007/task (arXiv:2608.09888). Thinks in latent space, no chain-of-thought essays — cheap, not champion. Self-reported, weights unreleased.

Don't leave with the wrong lesson (we almost did): this forgetting is silent — no "sorry, forgot" flag. And remembering for one chat ≠ learning forever. Getting fast memory into slow weights is still unsolved. Also please, never call BDH an SSM in front of the judges.
